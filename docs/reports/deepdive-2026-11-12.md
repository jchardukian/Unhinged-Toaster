## What separates top from bottom (first 2 seconds inspected)
- Top (Pumpkin, Kitten, Armadillo): subject close and large in frame 0, something about to happen; Kitten's
  headline is a countdown (suspense). Armadillo had the best retention (44%) and also led YouTube (35 views).
- Bottom (Ball pit, Kids, Llama): small/far subjects or letterboxed footage with the source's own text,
  label headlines ("… IN ACTION!", "… YES, PLEASE!"), commentary that narrates ("Look at those fluffy speedsters go!").
- Average watch time is ~3–13 s regardless of length, so 30–58 s clips are mostly abandoned (10–20% watched);
  the 7 s clip was watched 82%. Length is a lever, but the sample is too small to change the length rules yet.
- QC score (7.2–7.6 on everything) does not separate winners from losers.

Key findings:
- TeamYouTube 2026-10-01 (support.google.com/youtube/blog/470890423): channels "primarily aggregating or
  re-uploading" content "will likely see less distribution". YouTube counts original commentary, storytelling and
  unique edits; it does NOT count voice-over descriptions of what is on screen or template-based bulk changes.
  This is the most likely reason YouTube reach is weaker than TikTok's for this channel, beyond the cold start.
- The fastest-growing comparable (AniRank) adds a strong personal POV (rankings, reactions), not narration.
- Swipe stoppers with official backing: value in the first seconds, suspense/surprise hooks, text on screen from
  frame 0, length only as long as the moment needs, sound on.

## Change made (PR "Deep dive 2026-11-12")
Copywriter (strict hook rules, live with the clean template):
1. Headlines must build suspense/curiosity and never be topic labels; label patterns are rejected and retried.
2. Commentary must be the toaster's own verdict/joke; narrating openers ("Look at", "This little…") are rejected and retried.
3. A caption that is still generic after the retry is repaired (generic sentence dropped, or the hook headline used),
   so "Watch this…" can no longer be published.
Expected effect: fewer swipes in the first second (hook) and more original contribution (YouTube eligibility).
